Purpose of ITR-4



Purpose of ITR-4

The purpose of ITR-4 is to:

  • Simplify tax compliance for small taxpayers.
  • Facilitate presumptive taxation for individuals and entities.
  • Reduce the burden of maintaining exhaustive financial records.

Applicability of ITR-4

ITR-4 applies to the following taxpayers who meet specific conditions:

Eligible Taxpayers:

  • Individuals, HUFs, and Firms (excluding LLPs) opting for presumptive taxation under:
    • Section 44AD for businesses.
    • Section 44ADA for professions.
    • Section 44AE for transporters.
  • Total income during the financial year does not exceed ₹50 lakhs.
  • Income sources limited to:
    • Salary or pension.
    • Income from one house property (excluding losses brought forward).
    • Income from other sources, such as interest and family pension.
    • Income from eligible presumptive business or profession.

Presumptive Taxation: Simplified Taxation Scheme

Presumptive taxation is a scheme under the Income Tax Act, 1961, designed to simplify the tax compliance process for small businesses, professionals, and transport operators. Instead of maintaining detailed accounting records and undergoing audits, taxpayers under this scheme can declare income at a prescribed percentage of their gross receipts or turnover.

Section 44AD (Business Income):

  • For eligible businesses with gross turnover/receipts up to ₹2 crores.
  • Presumptive income: 8% of turnover (6% for digital transactions).

Section 44ADA (Professional Income):

  • For specified professions with gross receipts up to ₹50 lakhs.
  • Presumptive income: 50% of gross receipts.

Section 44AE (Income from Goods Carriages):

  • For taxpayers owning up to 10 goods carriages.
  • Presumptive income: ₹7,500 per month per vehicle.

Non-Applicability of ITR-4


ITR-4 is not applicable to taxpayers who:

  1. Are directors in a company.
  2. Hold unlisted equity shares.
  3. Have assets or signing authority in accounts outside India.
  4. Have income from:
    • More than one house property.
    • Capital gains.
    • Speculative income (e.g., lottery winnings).
  5. Claim relief under Sections 90, 90A, or 91 (tax treaties).
  6. Require income adjustments due to Section 5A (Portuguese Civil Code).
  7. Have agricultural income exceeding ₹5,000.
  8. Need to carry forward or set off losses.

Structure of the ITR-4 Form



The ITR-4 form is divided into four parts for easy reporting of income and tax details:

Part A: General Information

  • Basic details:
    • Name, PAN, Aadhaar number, and contact details.
    • Filing status (original or revised return).
    • Nature of business/profession.
    • Opt-in/opt-out status under presumptive taxation.

Part B: Gross Total Income

  • Reporting of income from:
    • Salary/Pension.
    • House Property: Limited to one property.
    • Other Sources: Interest, family pension, etc.
    • Business/Profession: Presumptive income under Sections 44AD, 44ADA, or 44AE.

Part C: Deductions and Total Taxable Income

  • Deduction claims under various sections of Chapter VI-A:
    • 80C: Investments in PPF, LIC, NSC, etc.
    • 80D: Health insurance premiums.
    • 80G: Donations to eligible institutions.
    • 80E: Interest on education loans.
  • Total taxable income is calculated after applying deductions.

Part D: Tax Computation and Status

  • Tax computation includes:
    • Total tax liability.
    • Advance tax payments.
    • TDS/TCS deductions.
    • Refunds or additional tax payable.

Supplementary Schedules

  • Schedule BP: Presumptive income details under Sections 44AD, 44ADA, or 44AE.
  • Schedule IT: Details of advance tax and self-assessment tax payments.
  • Schedule TCS: Taxes collected at source.
  • Schedule TDS: Taxes deducted at source (salary and other income).



    Key Features of Presumptive Taxation

    Presumptive taxation is a simplified tax scheme designed to ease compliance for small businesses and professionals.

  • a. Section 44AD: Business Income
    • Eligible for:
      • Resident Individuals, HUFs, and Firms (excluding LLPs).
      • Businesses with a turnover up to ₹2 crores.
    • Presumed income:
      • 8% of turnover/receipts (6% for digital transactions).
    • Not applicable for:
      • Commission or brokerage businesses.
      • Agency businesses.
  • b. Section 44ADA: Professional Income
    • Eligible for specified professions, such as:
      • Legal, medical, engineering, architecture, and accounting.
    • Presumed income: 50% of gross receipts.
    • Gross receipts should not exceed ₹50 lakhs.
  • c. Section 44AE: Transporters
    • Applies to businesses owning up to 10 goods carriages.
    • Presumptive income: ₹7,500 per month per vehicle.


    Documents Required for ITR-4 Filing

  • 1. PAN Card.
  • 2. Aadhaar Card.
  • 3. Bank account details (account number and IFSC).
  • 4. Proof of presumptive income (e.g., turnover/receipts).
  • 5. Details of tax deducted at source (TDS).
  • 6. Investment proofs for deductions under Chapter VI-A.


    Filing Deadlines

    Non-audit cases: July 31 of the assessment year.
    Audit cases (if applicable): October 31 of the assessment year.

    Penalties for Late Filing of ITR-4

  • 1. Late Filing Penalty Under Section 234F:
    • If the return is filed after the due date but before 31st December, a penalty of ₹5,000 will be levied.
    • If the total income is below ₹5 lakh, the penalty is reduced to ₹1,000.
  • 2. Interest Under Section 234A:
    • Interest is charged at 1% per month on the outstanding tax if the taxpayer has failed to pay taxes by the due date.
  • 3. Other Penalties:
    • A penalty of up to ₹5,000 under Section 271F may be imposed for failure to file the return within the prescribed time, even after the extended due date.


Steps for Filing ITR-4


Follow these steps to file the ITR-4 form effectively:

  • Gather required documents.
  • Log in to the Income Tax e-filing portal.
  • Select ITR-4 and choose the appropriate assessment year.
  • Fill in all parts of the form:
    • Personal and income details.
    • Tax deduction claims.
    • Advance tax and TDS details.
  • Verify the form online using Aadhaar OTP or DSC.
  • Submit and download the acknowledgment.

Common Mistakes to Avoid


Avoid these common errors while filing ITR-4:

  • Filing ITR-4 when not eligible.
  • Incorrect declaration of presumptive income.
  • Omitting TDS or advance tax payments.
  • Missing investment proofs for deductions.
