Professional Tax Registration Certificate and Compliance

Professional tax is a direct tax levied on persons earning an income by either practicing a profession, employment, calling, or trade. Unlike income tax imposed by the Central Government, professional tax is levied by the government of a state or union territory in India. In the case of salaried and wage earners, the professional tax is liable to be deducted by the Employer from the salary/wages, and the same is to be deposited to the state government. In the case of other classes of individuals, this tax is liable to be paid by the employee himself. The tax calculation and amount collected may vary from one State to another, but it has a maximum limit of Rs. 2500/- per year.

Professional Tax (PT) is a state-level tax levied on individuals earning income through employment, trade, calling, or profession. The tax serves as a source of revenue for state governments to fund local welfare and development programs. It is regulated by Article 276 of the Indian Constitution, allowing states to legislate and impose professional tax.

  • Maximum Cap: ₹2,500 per annum.
  • Applicability: Levied on income earners, including salaried employees and self-employed professionals.

Unlike Income Tax (a central government levy), professional tax is a state government levy. Each state has its own rules regarding registration, rates, exemptions, and compliance.

Professional Tax Applicability


Professional Tax applies to the following:

For Salaried Individuals:
  • Employers deduct PT from employee salaries based on income slabs.
  • Employers deposit the collected tax to the state government.
For Self-Employed Individuals:
  • Individuals practicing professions (e.g., doctors, lawyers, chartered accountants) are liable to pay directly to the state government.
Entities Liable:
  • Companies.
  • Firms.
  • LLPs.
  • Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs).
  • Co-operative societies.
  • Associations or bodies of individuals (incorporated or not).

Exemptions from Professional Tax

The following individuals and entities are typically exempt:

  • 1. Senior citizens aged 65 years or above.
  • 2. Parents of children with permanent disabilities or mental illness.
  • 3. Individuals with permanent physical disabilities, including blindness.
  • 4. Members of the armed forces or reservists.
  • 5. Textile industry workers (in some states).
  • 6. Agents under specific government savings schemes like the Mahila Pradhan Kshetriya Bachat Yojana.

Registration and Compliance


For Employers:

  • 1. Register under the Professional Tax Act of the respective state.
  • 2. Obtain Professional Tax Registration Certificate (PTRC) for deducting PT from employee salaries.
  • 3. File periodic returns and deposit the collected tax.

For Self-Employed Professionals:

  • 1. Register for Professional Tax Enrollment Certificate (PTEC).
  • 2. Pay the applicable tax directly to the state government.

Documents Required for Registration

  • 1. PAN and Aadhaar of the individual/entity.
  • 2. Certificate of Incorporation (for companies/LLPs).
  • 3. Partnership deed (if applicable).
  • 4. Address proof of business.
  • 5. Salary details of employees (for employers).
  • 6. Bank account details.
  • 7. Proof of professional qualifications (if self-employed).

Payment and Filing Deadlines

  • • Payment: Monthly, quarterly, or annually, depending on state regulations.
  • • Filing Returns: Filing deadlines differ for each state. Employers must file returns within prescribed timeframes.
Example:
  • In Maharashtra:
  • • Annual payment deadline for self-employed individuals: March 31.
  • • Monthly or quarterly payment deadlines for employers.

Penalties for Non-Compliance

  • 1. Penalties for Late Registration: ₹5 per day in many states (e.g., Maharashtra).
  • 2. Late Payment Penalty:
    • • Interest: 1.25% per month.
    • • Penalty: 10% of the tax amount.
  • 3. Late Filing of Returns: ₹1,000 or more depending on the state.
  • 4. Criminal Proceedings: In severe cases, non-compliance may lead to legal action.




    Benefits of Professional Tax Compliance

    1. Avoidance of Legal Hassles: Timely registration and payment prevent penalties and legal complications.
    2. Contribution to State Welfare: Revenue collected supports state welfare and development programs.
    3. Tax Deductions: Employers can claim professional tax paid as a business expense.
    4. Smooth Business Operations: Proper compliance enhances credibility with regulatory authorities.


    Step-by-Step Registration Process

    1. Submit an application to the state tax department with requisite documents.
    2. Tax authorities verify the application and documents.
    3. Obtain a Professional Tax Certificate (PTRC or PTEC) upon approval.


    Common Mistakes in Professional Tax Compliance

    1. Delayed registration.
    2. Incorrect deduction of tax amounts.
    3. Failure to update salary changes in filings.
    4. Missing deadlines for payments and returns.
